Research

SiMn Slag Valorization & Peat Stabilization

Investigated granulated (GS) and dry (DS) silicomanganese slag as secondary materials for cement-assisted stabilization of highly organic peat.

  • Used a 23 full-factorial design, extended mixtures with 50–70% total slag, and grading-matched silica sand controls.
  • At 28 days, slag mixtures developed approximately 2.4–2.7 times the unconfined compressive strength (UCS) of their corresponding sand controls.
  • Connected strength development with hydration products, leaching chemistry, and life-cycle greenhouse gas emissions.
  • By-product valorization
  • Peat stabilization
  • Design of experiments
Mechanical

Strength Development & Mix Optimization

Measured UCS after 3, 7, 14, and 28 days and evaluated mixtures against an adopted 200 kPa screening target.

  • The highest measured 28-day UCS was approximately 1,980 kPa (M11: 40% GS, 30% DS, 15% cement, and 15% peat).
  • Factorial analysis identified cement as the dominant strength factor, followed by DS, with a significant DS–cement interaction.
  • Local interpolation with a one-sided 95% lower confidence bound identified a rounded mix of 25% GS, 20% DS, 15% cement, and 40% peat.
  • For that estimated mix, fitted mean UCS was 228.2 kPa and the lower confidence bound was 214.5 kPa. The 200 kPa target is a study screening benchmark.
  • UCS
  • ANOVA
  • Statistical optimization
Characterization

Hydration Products & Microstructure

Combined X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and thermogravimetric analysis–differential scanning calorimetry (TGA–DSC) to interpret strength development.

  • XRD and thermal analysis supported progressive portlandite consumption during curing.
  • SEM showed a transition from a porous matrix to denser coatings and interparticle bridges, with features consistent with C-S-H and ettringite-type products.
  • The combined observations were consistent with continued hydration and pozzolanic reactions accompanying the strength gain.
  • XRD
  • SEM
  • TGA–DSC
  • Hydration mechanisms
Environmental

Leaching Chemistry & Environmental Screening

Assessed six 28-day cured mixtures using BS EN 12457-2 batch leaching and ICP-OES, supported by PHREEQC geochemical modeling.

  • At 40–70% slag, eluate Mn was 0.30–0.55 mg/L, compared with 1.08 mg/L for the slag-free peat–cement control; at most about 0.011% of the slag Mn inventory was released.
  • Eluate pH was 11.51–11.91; modeling was consistent with C-S-H buffering in most slag mixtures.
  • Cu release exceeded the EU inert-waste screening limit in four of five slag mixtures. Mn and Cu exceeded Malaysian Standard A in five and four mixtures, respectively; both met Standard B and Japanese national effluent screening limits.
  • Raw-slag TCLP and total-content results met the compared US EPA toxicity-characteristic levels and TTLC values for measured parameters. Batch results are screening evidence; long-term behavior and Cd/Pb detection limits require further evaluation.
  • BS EN 12457-2
  • ICP-OES
  • PHREEQC
  • TCLP
Sustainability

Life-Cycle Greenhouse Gas Assessment

Compared cradle-to-site greenhouse gas emissions per tonne of peat stabilized, with sensitivity analyses for slag transport and allocation of production impacts.

  • Under the base-case cut-off approach, the estimated slag mix (Case A) emitted 186 kg CO2e per tonne of peat.
  • This was 30% below the sand–cement alternative (265 kg CO2e) and 51% below the tested 45% cement–peat mix (377 kg CO2e). The latter reached only 114 kPa, below the 200 kPa target.
  • The carbon advantage over the sand alternative persisted up to approximately 770 km of slag transport under the study assumptions.
  • The result depends on the functional unit and allocation assumptions: per tonne of stabilized mixture, Case A and the sand alternative had the same base-case emissions.
